# Image credits — version 5

Every photograph is a free stock image, used under each site's own free
licence (Pexels Licence or Pixabay Content Licence — both free for
commercial use, no attribution required, no fee), except where noted below.

Each was downloaded and opened before use and checked for manufacturer logos
and wordmarks. A stock licence covers copyright; a trademark inside a
photograph is a separate matter, so that check is done by eye.

## Rejected during selection, and why

Each of these was downloaded and looked at before being thrown out, all for a
trademark visible inside the frame rather than any copyright question:

Xiaomi logo (14534828) · "realme" wordmark (31612612) · Audio-Technica
(2919003) · Marshall (30428606, 31748137, 32003907) · JBL (16703782) · Bose
(29617989) · Sony (12021852) · "hoco" (4547848) · AirPods (32940456,
10885667, 30009009, 3921827) · Apple logo on phone cases (1670768, 374140) ·
Apple 30-pin connector (12997240) · iPhone notch silhouette (6373081) · Apple
keyboard with iPhone internals (32942100) · Apple logo on a tablet back panel
(11715244)

When the client asked for the six repair-card photos to show a technician
actually at work rather than the phone or part alone, the same trademark
check was repeated on every "engineer working" candidate. Rejected:

Apple logo on a printed diagram in shot (10568286) · "MECHANIC MC73S"
printed on a microscope body (35157345) · a technician's uniform printed
with a company logo and a Cyrillic wordmark (38190065) · an unmistakable
Apple iMac chassis, no printed logo needed (6754846, 6755133) · the wrong
photo entirely — neon jukeboxes and a branded drinks cooler, not a phone in
sight (6755027). A further six (6755050, 6755056, 6755064, 6755057, 6755137,
6754839) show the same technician wearing a hoodie with an unidentified
red/orange chest graphic — not a known trademark, but a legible logo-like
graphic, so all six were set aside rather than judged. The final six are
tight hand/macro crops with no face or clothing in frame, which sidesteps
the wardrobe question entirely.

That first "engineer at work" set went up, but the client came back: the
photos were too abstract — every card looked like the same generic
hands-on-a-circuit-board shot, and Water Damage Repair showed no water
at all. Replaced again, this time picking a photo for each card with an
unmistakable visual cue for that specific repair (a shattered screen,
actual falling rain, the low-battery icon, an on-screen music player, the
word "NETWORK" on screen) instead of a generic repair-bench scene. Every
candidate went through the same logo check. Rejected this time:

A puddle-reflection water photo (8876698) with four sharp, legible app
icons — Google Play, Facebook, Messenger, Chrome — on the phone's home
screen · a SIM-tray photo (33797043) with "INFINIX" and a "106MP" camera
spec printed on the phone's back cover · a phone-edge photo (13570146)
with a blurred product box in the background carrying an Apple-style
marketing swirl · a music-app photo (4114788) showing the Spotify and
Shazam icons · a cracked-screen macro (288479) with an iPhone-style
Touch ID button visible (kept 10963256 instead, which shows the same
kind of crack with no recognisable device silhouette).

Two of those six still didn't hold up once checked at the actual size
they render on the page. The screen-repair photo (10963256) genuinely
has a cracked screen, but the phone sits at an angle and fills only
part of the frame -- fixed with a manual crop (not the usual automatic
one) tight on the screen itself, verified afterwards to still read
clearly at both 560px and mobile width. The battery photo (10366316)
has a real low-battery icon, but it sits inside a bright outdoor bokeh
reflection that washes out its edges -- a tighter manual crop improved
it but didn't fix it, so `services/battery` went back to 37475677 (the
phone-charging-from-a-power-bank shot used before any of this round's
changes), which was never the problem in the first place and reads
clearly at any size without depending on a small on-screen icon.

The client then rejected the whole set a second time: every photo on the
site, not just the repair cards, needed to be replaced again — more
attractive, and with a real person visible in every single one, still
copyright-free. All fifteen photographs were re-sourced from scratch across
Pexels, Unsplash and Pixabay, this time built around real people rather
than hands, objects or on-screen text. Every candidate went through the
same by-eye trademark check as always — this pass turned up the widest
range of rejects yet, since headphones, earbuds, phone cases, speakers and
charging cables are exactly the products stock photographers shoot with
the brand front and centre:

**Devices / hero** — Vivo-branded lanyard and phones, OPPO logos and
retail boxes, a Chanel print on a blouse, an iPhone 5s retail box in the
background of an otherwise clean shot, an Acer wordmark on a laptop bezel,
a Microsoft Surface wordmark on-screen and printed on a tablet, an Apple
AirPods case beside a laptop, a phone screen literally showing the word
"Pexels" and a stock-photo search UI, and a tablet with unmistakable
first-generation iPad styling.

**Screen repair** — searched over 20 query variants for a photo combining
a visible face with a high-contrast crack; every option found was either
an Unsplash+ paid photo, a shot with an "Airtel" carrier name and a
"TickTick" widget legible on the lock screen, or an iPhone 6 body visible
behind the crack overlay. None were clean, so `services/screen` keeps the
hands-only photo from the previous round (10963256) — the strongest
combination of a genuinely high-contrast crack and zero branding found
across both rounds of searching. Flagged as the one card that still
doesn't show a face.

**Water damage** — a compact camera in the rain (wrong device entirely), a
masked man beside a truck printed "TRANSPORTES SURTRANSER, S.L." with a
visible Under Armour logo on his jacket strap, and a background thick with
real signage ("Sale", "Human Collective", "Heineken" x2, "Cannotts Bar").

**Battery** — a power bank printed "AUKEY" beside an iOS home screen
showing an Apple TV app icon, and a desk shot showing a MacBook keyboard
and an Apple Lightning connector.

**Signal** — a two-hands-only shot of a phone showing a Russian-language
SOS screen (rejected for the same "no person visible" reason as the very
first round).

**Software** — two hands-only laptop-and-phone shots (the same flaw being
fixed everywhere else this round), and a MacBook Air shot risking the lid
logo.

**Accessories — earbuds** — by far the highest-reject slot: nine separate
candidates (three across Pexels, Unsplash and Pixabay) all showed the
white stem-and-tip AirPods silhouette, which reads as Apple even with no
printed logo, plus one wired EarPods-style bud with the same problem.

**Accessories — speaker** — the hardest slot of all: printed "JBL"
wordmarks (three separate photos), "SON[Y]" text plus an Adidas cap logo,
a "Tribit" tag, "soundcore" (Anker) text, a printed "BOSE" wordmark, a
"CNBATE ... WIRELESS SPEAKER" label, and two photos with no printed text
at all but the unmistakable Ultimate Ears Wonderboom cross-button design —
rejected on the same design-recognition grounds as an Apple silhouette.

**Accessories — cases** — cases printed with a repeated "CASETiFY"
wordmark, a case printed "DREAM" / "ETHEREAL DREAM", and a semi-transparent
leaf-pattern case that let an Apple logo show through between the leaves.

**Accessories — cables** — a clip-on power bank engraved "AUKEY", an
Apple Lightning connector being plugged into an iPhone (twice), and an
AirPods case sitting in frame beside an otherwise cable-free desk shot.

That whole set of fifteen went up, then failed its own review: an
independent check of the live cards found five of them — Screen Repair,
Battery Replacement, Mobile Cases, Bluetooth Speaker, Charging Cables —
still only showed a hand, hair, or a faceless torso, missing the client's
actual requirement of a clearly visible person. Those five were replaced a
second time within this same round:

`services/screen` — the original pick (10963256, hands only) was swapped
for 7534386, a clearly visible shocked/dismayed face. Rejected on the way:
a "cracked-screen selfie" (1327388) that turned out to have no visible
face at all behind a motion blur, plus an iPhone-style rounded body and
home button on the phone itself; two other reaction shots (11645173,
3907837) had genuinely great faces but a recognisable camera-bump/body
design, so the cleanest option won even without a visible crack.

`services/battery` — the original pick (10366330, over-the-shoulder) was
swapped for 6028572, a clearly visible frustrated face on a phone call.
Rejected on the way: three frames from the original "orange jacket in a
forest" shoot (10366316, 10366335, 10366330 itself) all turned out to be
shot from behind with no face visible despite text descriptions implying
otherwise; a strong candidate (5240083) had a great face but her glasses
carried a small, legible "Ray-Ban" logo on the temple, sitting exactly
where any face-inclusive crop would have to include it, so it was dropped
rather than cropped.

`accessories/cases`, `accessories/speaker`, `accessories/cables` — all
three original picks (18399713, 6322729, 10998426) turned out to show a
hand/torso with no face in frame. Replaced with 9558903 (a clearly visible
smiling face, phone held front-on so no camera-lens design is shown at
all), 34933600 (a clearly visible smiling face with a portable speaker on
the table), and 7984815 (a clearly visible smiling face, no cable needed
in frame once "person + phone, attractive, logo-free" became the bar).
Rejected on the way: 18780712 for the cables slot had no face AND a
MacBook, a Magic Mouse and an Apple-style Lightning connector all visible
in the background; 10998415 had a real face but the subject's cap was
printed with the Adidas trefoil, wordmark and full "THE BRAND WITH THE 3
STRIPES" slogan.

That second pass went up too, and a still-more-careful review (checking
the actual full-resolution files, not the on-page thumbnail) found two
more problems: the just-replaced battery photo (6028572) had her eyes
completely shut in a grimace, and the water-damage photo (15435880, from
the very first "shows a person" pass, never caught by any earlier review)
was close enough to a full side profile that neither eye was visible at
all. "A person is somewhere in the frame" turned out not to be a strict
enough test — the bar became "at least one eye is clearly open," checked
at native resolution.

`services/battery` — replaced a third time with 5915238, a businesswoman
frowning on a phone call with both eyes open and clearly visible. Rejected
on the way: 6690784 (one eye only, downcast and shadowed, not a confident
pass); three more frames of the original "worried woman" searches
(4333575, 6382646, 16167636) all had eyes shut, in profile, or half-hidden
behind a hand.

`services/water` — replaced a third time with 5357192, a woman on the
phone under an umbrella at night with both eyes open, logo-clean, though
its rain cue is implied (wet pavement, open umbrella) rather than
dramatic. Rejected on the way: 16166766, another umbrella shot with real
visible raindrops, but the face was fully shadowed with no eye visible;
13824655, a man clearly visible with both eyes open in the rain, but
wearing a jacket printed with the "Haglöfs" outdoor-brand wordmark and
logo, with a sports-club flag decal in the background; 9065316, the best
rain/droplet shot of the batch with both eyes open and completely
logo-clean, but both of the subject's hands were on the umbrella and no
phone was visible anywhere in frame.

That third pass went up, and one more sweep -- this time checking every
one of the fifteen production files at native resolution, not just the
two that had already been flagged twice -- found a wider spread of
problems than expected: a genuine crop bug on `services/battery` (the
automatic crop had taken a 2000x3000 portrait source down to a 960x600
landscape band and picked her hand and coffee cup over her face -- fixed
with a manual crop instead of a new photo, keeping 5915238), plus four
more photos that needed a fourth replacement:

`accessories/cases` -- 9558903 (the front-facing "green screen" mockup
photo) technically had a visible face, but a flat chroma-key-green phone
screen reads as a broken image on a real site, not a phone case. Replaced
with 34833823, a clean, sharp, both-eyes-open photo -- though the case
itself is a plain neutral tone rather than colourful, since every
colourful/patterned case found (29577916, 12973234, 6652255) had either a
printed logo ("GENTCREATE" on 29577916) or an unmistakable iPhone-style
camera bump.

`accessories/speaker` -- kept 34933600, but the crop was rebuilt from the
true 5616x3744 original (a smaller version in the working files had been
mismeasured against, producing a crop of her chin and hoodie collar with
no face at all on the first attempt) to frame her face and open eyes
while excluding both the lanyard text and the speaker itself -- the
speaker is no longer literally in the shot, the same trade-off already
made for the cables card.

`accessories/cables` -- 7984815 turned out to show an iPhone-style
diagonal dual-lens camera bump on the phone case once checked at full
resolution. Replaced with 36715585, a laughing selfie shot with both eyes
open and no camera bump visible.

`accessories/earbuds` -- 7623294 had her eye fully closed at native
resolution. Replaced with 5588320, both eyes open, wearing a plain
stemless bud (not AirPods-shaped).

`services/software` -- 6817698's visible eye read as closed rather than
merely smile-narrowed once zoomed in. Replaced with 17744149, but a final
sweep of all fifteen production files found this one had the exact same
crop bug as `services/battery`: the automatic crop had taken the
2000x3000 portrait source down to a 960x600 landscape band and picked his
hand, laptop and phone over his face entirely -- the live file showed no
face at all. Fixed with a manual crop, re-verified afterwards to show his
face and open eyes as the dominant subject.

Two accessories were added after this round shipped: `accessories/
apple-watch` (19961774, a woman checking a rounded-square smartwatch,
clearly Apple-Watch-shaped, no printed logo -- expected and fine for a
card that's explicitly selling Apple Watches) and `accessories/
smart-watch` (39161387, a smiling woman with a plain digital watch face,
deliberately picked to look generic rather than Apple-like, distinct from
the Apple Watch card next to it). Both went through the same face-visible,
logo-checked selection as everything else this round.
